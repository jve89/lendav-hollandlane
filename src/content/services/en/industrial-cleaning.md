## The documents you get before the work starts

On an industrial site the first question is usually what the person responsible
for safety can put in their file. We send you, in writing:

- a copy of the operational authorisation,
- proof of liability insurance,
- a site-specific risk assessment.

They go out before the work starts, so there is time to pass them on and read
them. Ask for them with the quote and they arrive in the same email as the price.

Nobody stands on the structure and we put up no scaffolding — the crew stays on
the ground.

## How we scope the site

We do not price an industrial job before we have scoped it with you. Write to us
and tell us:

- what the building or structure is, and how tall;
- what the surface to be cleaned is made of;
- how the site is reached, and what surrounds it;
- whether the work can run during production, or needs a shutdown window;
- the address.

From that we tell you whether and how we would take the work on, what it covers
and what it does not. The price comes after that, and it comes in writing.

## The structures worth asking about

The list below says which structures we discuss — not what has already been done.
If yours is one of these, or close to one, it is worth asking:

- factories and production buildings
- warehouses and logistics sites
- silos and tanks
- sports halls
- agricultural buildings
- large commercial solar arrays
- signage and cladding at height
- ships
- wind turbines

If your structure is not on the list, ask anyway. We will say plainly if it is not
work for us.

## What this service does not cover

**Cleaning is not an inspection.** We produce no report, assessment or certificate
on the condition of a structure, a roof or a piece of equipment, and a clean is
not a substitute for a survey. If you need a report on the condition of the site,
say so separately in your enquiry — that is different work and we answer it
separately.

We do no repairs. We do not fix damaged sheeting, loose fixings or failed
penetrations. If we see any while cleaning, we tell you.

We do no electrical work, no cleaning of process equipment, and no interior
cleaning.

If the structure turns out not to suit drone cleaning, we say so at the scoping
stage, not after the job.

## How the price is reached

Roof and facade cleaning have a published square-metre price. Industrial work does
not, and that is not an omission.

What decides the price on a large structure is access, height, the surface and the
shutdown window, not the area. Two buildings with the same surface area can be
completely different jobs if one can be washed during production and the other
cannot. A square-metre price here would produce a number that was wrong in your
favour or in ours.

So the price comes after scoping, in writing, and it is binding.

## When the work happens

The shutdown window and the working times are agreed at the scoping stage and
written into the quote. We publish no general duration: it depends on the site,
and we have no measurements of our own to stand on yet.

The weather applies here as everywhere else: we do not fly in strong wind, rain or
sub-zero temperatures. If the weather postpones the work, we agree a new date.
